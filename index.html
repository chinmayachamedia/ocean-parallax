0–200M · SUNLIGHT ZONE

The epipelagic

Enough light reaches here for photosynthesis, so this thin skin of water holds the vast majority of marine life — plankton, tuna, dolphins, and the coral reefs built by animals that farm sunlight for a living. It is the only layer with a visible horizon.

90%of ocean life lives here
1 atmpressure added per 10m

200–1,000M · TWILIGHT ZONE

The mesopelagic

Sunlight thins to a blue-grey gloom and then to nothing. Most animals here make their own light instead of waiting for the sun — roughly nine in ten mesopelagic species are bioluminescent, using flashes to hunt, hide, and speak.

~1%of surface light remains at 200m
90%of species here glow in the dark

1,000–4,000M · MIDNIGHT ZONE

The bathypelagic

No sunlight has ever reached this deep. The water sits near freezing, pressure exceeds 400 atmospheres, and food arrives only as "marine snow" drifting down from above. Animals here are built to wait, not chase.

4,000–6,000M · ABYSSAL ZONE

The abyssopelagic

This is the ocean floor for most of the planet — flat sediment plains broken by the occasional hydrothermal vent, where life runs on chemistry pulled from the earth instead of energy from the sun.

6,000–11,000M · HADAL ZONE

The trenches

Named for Hades, these narrow trenches are the deepest scars on Earth's surface. The Mariana Trench alone could swallow Everest with two kilometres of water left over — and still, something lives down there.
